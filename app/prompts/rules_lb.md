# Luxembourgish rules sheet

Rule sheet, not LOD: a hand-written summary of secondary sources, not reviewed by a native
speaker. It covers spelling and sentence structure. It gives no word forms: conjugations,
participles, auxiliaries, plurals and n-rule forms of a word come from the "LOD facts" block.

## 1. Eifeler Regel (n-rule) · rule sheet, not LOD

- A final -n is dropped before a consonant: `den Ball` becomes `de Ball`. It applies to the
  endings -(e)n and -nn and to function words (articles, pronouns, prepositions, conjunctions,
  adverbs).
- The -n stays before a vowel (`den Apel`), before n, t, d, ts, tsch, dsch and h
  (`den Noper`, `den Dag`, `den Hond`), and before a pause.
- Before a function word that starts with s (`si`, `se`, `sech`, `sou`) dropping is optional.
- The prefixes on- and in- keep their n.
- Exceptions named by the source (the n in parentheses is the one concerned): `Wäi(n)`,
  `Stee(n)`, `geschwë(nn)`.
- The spelling shows the rule: write the word without the n where it is dropped. A loanword
  plural takes a diaeresis: `Chance`, plural `Chancen`, without the n `Chancë`.

## 2. Word order · rule sheet, not LOD

- Main clause: the finite verb is the second element.
- Yes/no question and imperative: the finite verb comes first.
- Subordinate clause: the finite verb comes last.
- Non-finite verbs gather at the end of the clause. Two infinitives may come in either order:
  `kënne kommen` or `komme kënnen`.

## 3. Gender and case · rule sheet, not LOD

- Three genders: masculine, feminine, neuter. The plural has no gender.
- Three cases: nominative, accusative, dative.
- No real genitive; only remnants such as `Enn des Mounts`. Possession is dative plus
  possessive: `dem Mann säi Buch`.

## 4. Past tense · rule sheet, not LOD

- The everyday past is the perfect: `hunn` or `sinn` plus the past participle, with the
  participle at the end of the clause.
- There is no progressive form.
- The preterite survives for a few frequent verbs (`sinn`, `hunn`, `wëssen`); otherwise the
  preterite and the subjunctive are obsolete in general Luxembourgish.
- Rule of thumb, seen in dictionary entries and not stated as a rule by any source: some
  verbs take `sinn` (`goen`, `ukommen`), others take `hunn` (`maachen`, `ausginn`).

## 5. Irregular and separable verbs · rule sheet, not LOD

- Verbs can be irregular. `goen` changes its vowel in the 2nd and 3rd person singular:
  `gees`, `geet`.
- Rule of thumb, seen in a dictionary entry and not stated as a rule by any source: a
  separable verb splits in the present: `ukommen` gives `du kënns un`.

## 6. Plural · rule sheet, not LOD

- Rule of thumb: the plural is often marked by an ending only, e.g. `Kand`, plural `Kanner`.
  The plural classes are not on this sheet.
